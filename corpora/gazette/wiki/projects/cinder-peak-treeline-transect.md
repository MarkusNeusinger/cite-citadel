# Cinder Peak treeline transect

The Cinder Peak treeline transect is a fixed line of thirty-six plots that the Meridian Gazette environment desk has followed since the transect was established.[^s1] The second-season report records its second full walk.[^s1] Every station is revisited twice a season, at the opening of the melt and again at its close.[^s1] The next walk is planned for next season on the same schedule.[^s2]

## Method

- Stand age at a station is the ring count of the oldest cored stem.[^s3]
- Stem density is counted over a fixed ten-by-ten-metre plot, living standing stems only; deadwood is tallied separately.[^s3]
- Canopy closure is measured by densiometer, averaged over four cardinal readings taken from the plot centre.[^s3]
- Each plot was photographed from its north-west corner on both visits; the photographs are archived with the survey and not reproduced in the report.[^s3]
- The report states that nothing in it is interpolated.[^s1]

Per-station values are in the [station registry](../registries/station-registry-cinder-peak-treeline-transect.md). The stations climb from 1197 m (T-01, Larch Hollow) to 1792 m (T-36, Lantern Edge).[^s3][^s4]

## Finding

The desk reports that the season's clearest pattern was canopy closure falling with elevation while stand age did not, and reads this as recruitment failing at the upper stations rather than the older stands there dying back.[^s2] This is the desk's own interpretation, attributed to it.
