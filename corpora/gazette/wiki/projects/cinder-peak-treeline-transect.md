The Cinder Peak Treeline Transect is a fixed line of thirty-six plots that [The Meridian
Gazette](../organizations/the-meridian-gazette.md)'s environment desk has followed since the
transect was established.[^s1] Every station is revisited twice a season — once at the
opening of the melt and again at its close — and each is reported in a fixed shape: position,
stand age from the oldest cored stem, stem density over the fixed plot, canopy closure, and
any damage found; a station with no reading is reported as having none.[^s1]

The desk's second full walk of the transect produced readings for all thirty-six stations,
recorded on the [treeline transect station registry](../registries/treeline-transect-station-registry-cinder-peak.md).[^s1]

Across the full transect, the season's clearest pattern was that canopy closure fell with
elevation while stand age did not.[^s2] The environment desk interprets this as recruitment
failing at the upper stations, rather than the older stands there dying back.[^s2] The desk
plans to walk the transect again next season on the same schedule.[^s2]
