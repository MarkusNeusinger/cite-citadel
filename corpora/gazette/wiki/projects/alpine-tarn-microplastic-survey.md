R. Halvorsen, M. Okonkwo, and L. Feretti of the Institute for Mountain Hydrology surveyed
microplastic concentrations in three high-altitude alpine tarns over a single melt season,
reported in a preprint that has not been peer-reviewed.[^s1]

The survey drew triplicate 5-litre samples from each tarn at two-week intervals, vacuum-filtered
them onto 0.45-micrometre membranes, and counted particles by fluorescence microscopy after Nile
Red staining.[^s2]

Particle counts rose through the season at all three sites and peaked in late summer.[^s3] Black
Tarn was the most affected site, reaching 1,180 microplastic particles per litre — the season's
maximum[^s3] and, per the abstract, roughly six times the concentration at the least-affected
site.[^s4] Glass Tarn recorded the lowest counts of the three tarns.[^s3] The third tarn is not
named in the preprint.

The authors discuss atmospheric deposition as a likely pathway for the observed
accumulation.[^s5]
