# Microplastic accumulation in three alpine tarns

## Bibliographic anchor

The preprint "Microplastic accumulation in three alpine tarns" is by R. Halvorsen, M. Okonkwo, and L. Feretti of the Institute for Mountain Hydrology.[^s1] It is marked as a preprint that has not been peer reviewed.[^s1] The prepared text gives no publication year.

## Scope and methods

The authors surveyed microplastic concentrations in three high-altitude tarns over a single melt season.[^s2] Triplicate 5-litre samples were drawn from each tarn at two-week intervals. They were vacuum-filtered onto 0.45-micrometre membranes and counted by fluorescence microscopy after Nile Red staining.[^s3]

## Findings reported by the authors

- Particle counts rose through the season at all sites and peaked in late summer.[^s4]
- Black Tarn had the season maximum, 1,180 particles per litre, and Glass Tarn recorded the lowest counts.[^s4]
- The abstract describes Black Tarn as the most affected site, holding up to 1,180 microplastic particles per litre, and says this is roughly six times the concentration at the least affected site.[^s2] The results section does not give the least-affected site's figure, so the sixfold ratio rests on the abstract alone; the body text does not let a reader check it.[^s4]
- The authors say they discuss likely atmospheric-deposition pathways.[^s2] The extracted text contains no discussion section, so no pathway findings are recorded here.
