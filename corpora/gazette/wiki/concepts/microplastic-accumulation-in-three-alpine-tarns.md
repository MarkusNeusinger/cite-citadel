# Microplastic accumulation in three alpine tarns

## Bibliographic anchor

The paper is a preprint, not peer reviewed, by R. Halvorsen, M. Okonkwo and L. Feretti of the Institute for Mountain Hydrology.[^s1] The source states no publication date.

## Study design

The authors surveyed microplastic concentrations in three high-altitude tarns over a single melt season.[^s2] Triplicate 5-litre samples were drawn from each tarn at two-week intervals. They were vacuum-filtered onto 0.45-micrometre membranes and counted by fluorescence microscopy after Nile Red staining.[^s3]

## Findings

- Particle counts rose through the season at all sites and peaked in late summer.[^s4]
- Black Tarn had the highest concentration. Its 1,180 particles per litre was the season maximum.[^s4] The abstract describes this as roughly six times the concentration at the least affected site;[^s2] the results section names Glass Tarn as the site with the lowest counts but gives no figure for it.[^s4]
- The authors discuss likely atmospheric-deposition pathways.[^s2]

## References cited by the paper

The paper lists five references: a review of freshwater microplastics (Nowak et al., 2019), atmospheric transport of fibres to remote lakes (Reyes and Duman, 2020), Nile Red staining protocols for microplastic counts (Okafor, 2018), deposition gradients above the treeline (Vance et al., 2021), and a technical note on tarn sampling design by Feretti and Halvorsen (2022).[^s5] These are works the paper cites, not findings of this study.
