# Tardigrades (water bears)

Tardigrades, the microscopic invertebrates commonly called water bears, endure near-total desiccation by entering a dormant state called cryptobiosis; when the loss of water drives it, biologists specifically call it anhydrobiosis.[^s1]

## The dried "tun"

- As a water bear dries, it loses roughly 97 percent of its body water and pulls its legs in to form a shrunken, barrel-shaped "tun".[^s2]
- A dried tun can wait for years, and rehydration revives it within minutes to hours.[^s3]

## Protective chemistry

To survive, a tardigrade floods its cells with the sugar trehalose and with tardigrade-specific intrinsically disordered proteins ([TDPs](../abbreviations/tdp-tardigrade-specific-intrinsically-disordered-proteins.md)). These vitrify (turn glassy) as the animal dries, cradling proteins and membranes so they do not shatter.[^s4]

## Exposure to space

In 2007, in an experiment known as TARDIS aboard the European FOTON-M3 satellite, tardigrades were carried into low Earth orbit and exposed directly to the vacuum of space and the Sun's unfiltered ultraviolet radiation. Some survived the vacuum, and a number of those also survived the radiation and went on to reproduce after returning to Earth.[^s5]
