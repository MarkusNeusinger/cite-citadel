Tardigrades, commonly called water bears, are microscopic invertebrates that endure near-total
desiccation by entering [cryptobiosis](../concepts/cryptobiosis.md), a dormant state.[^s1]

As a water bear dries, it loses roughly 97 percent of its body water and pulls its legs in to
form a shrunken, barrel-shaped "tun".[^s2] To survive, it floods its cells with the sugar
trehalose and with tardigrade-specific intrinsically disordered proteins
([TDPs](../abbreviations/tdps-tardigrade-specific-intrinsically-disordered-proteins.md)), which
vitrify — turn glassy — as the animal dries, cradling its proteins and membranes so they do not
shatter.[^s3]

Tardigrades can survive direct exposure to the vacuum of space and the Sun's unfiltered
ultraviolet radiation, as demonstrated by the [TARDIS](../projects/tardis.md) experiment.[^s4]

A dried tun can wait for years, and rehydration revives it within minutes to hours.[^s5]
