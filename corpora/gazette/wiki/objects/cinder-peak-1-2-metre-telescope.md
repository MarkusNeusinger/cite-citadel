# Cinder Peak 1.2-metre telescope

[Cinder Peak Observatory](../organizations/cinder-peak-observatory.md) announced that its new 1.2-metre reflecting telescope achieved first light on 1 May 2026, capturing its first test images of the night sky.[^s1] It replaces the observatory's original 0.6-metre telescope, which had been in service since the observatory opened.[^s1]

## Capabilities

- The release states that the telescope roughly quadruples the observatory's light-gathering area.[^s2]
- It is fitted with a modern wide-field camera.[^s2]
- Staff astronomers say it will be used mainly for monitoring variable stars and for the observatory's long-running atmospheric-seeing survey, whose 2025 monthly medians appear in the [seeing brief](../concepts/atmospheric-seeing-at-cinder-peak-observatory.md).[^s2]

> [!CONTRADICTION]
> The press release says the new telescope roughly quadruples the light-gathering area [^s2], but the 2025 seeing brief says it offers about three times the light-gathering area of the retired 0.6-metre telescope [^s3].
> Resolution: Doubling the aperture from 0.6 m to 1.2 m multiplies the collecting area by four, so "roughly quadruples" is the consistent figure.[^llm1]

## Commissioning and use

- The observatory's director said, "First light is always the moment a telescope stops being a construction project and starts being an instrument."[^s4]
- Regular science observations are expected to begin after a commissioning period over the summer.[^s4]
- Public viewing nights were suspended while the dome was being resurfaced and are to resume on their published schedule once the observatory reopens to visitors.[^s5] The observatory's public notice dates the suspension 3 to 17 April 2026, with reopening to visitors on 18 April 2026.[^s6] See the [Public Open Night at Cinder Peak](../misc/public-open-night-at-cinder-peak.md), which offers a look through the telescope.
