Heike Brandt is Commercial Director at
[Blauwal Logistik GmbH](../organizations/blauwal-logistik-gmbh.md).[^s1] At the
[Projekt LEUCHTFEUER](../projects/projekt-leuchtfeuer.md) kickoff meeting she made the commercial
case for the programme: the money Blauwal pays every year merely to keep
[KOMET](../systems/komet.md)'s licences and support contracts alive buys no improvement of any
kind, and she committed to putting the exact figures in writing for the steering committee so the
business case is documented, not folklore.[^s2] She confirmed the Geschäftsführung approved a
programme budget of EUR 1.8 million, and was explicit that this is the whole envelope: "If we need
more, we go back to the Geschäftsführung — and nobody in this room wants that meeting."[^s3]

She kept that commitment: on 11 March 2024 she wrote to Marek Duszek's team with the figure —
KOMET's annual licence and support contracts stand at EUR 310,000, for a system whose vendor "has
not existed for seven years."[^s4]

At the steering committee meeting on 19 March 2024 she restated the budget framework — platform
licences, Gezeitenwerk's implementation services, internal personnel costs, training, and a
contingency reserve — and stated explicitly that exceeding it requires a fresh Geschäftsführung
decision, to be flagged early rather than after the fact.[^s5]

The programme charter names her the owner of Projekt LEUCHTFEUER's budget and vendor
commercials.[^s6]

At the steering committee's extraordinary session of 10 February 2025 she gave the commercial
rationale for reversing the database decision: under the KorallenDB vendor's revised licence terms
announced in December 2024, the persistence layer's five-year cost roughly doubles, with an audit
overhead nobody had priced in.[^s12] She also reported that the longer programme and the enlarged
interface package meant the original budget envelope no longer held; the Geschäftsführung approved
an increased programme budget of EUR 2.4 million on 16 January 2025, on the committee's
escalation, confirmed by the committee as decision D-11 at the same session.[^s13] By then she had
folded the increase into charter Revision B, issued by the PMO on 20 January 2025, and told the
committee the second envelope is to be treated as the last one: "There is no third ask in my
drawer."[^s14]

Her closing figures, reported in Petra Vogelsang's 20 March 2026 go-live announcement, put the
programme's final, actual spend at EUR 2.62 million against the EUR 2.4 million envelope — an
overrun she flagged to the Geschäftsführung in the autumn of 2025, with documented causes: the
customs certification delay and the extended dual-running of KOMET and QUAYSTONE account for
nearly all of it.[^s15] In the same announcement, Vogelsang thanked her "for envelopes defended and
honestly reported."[^s16]

On 15 April 2026 Brandt formally retracted her own memorandum of 10 June 2024, "KOMET operating
costs (provisional figures)."[^s17] The final operations audit for 2024–2025, accepted by the
Geschäftsführung on 31 March 2026, had closed the methodology the memo's downtime-cost estimate was
built on: the provisional approach double-counted contractual penalties the affected customer
accounts had in fact renegotiated, and extrapolated a peak-season hourly pattern across the whole
year, producing figures that differed materially, not marginally, from the audited ones.[^s18]

She instructed the programme not to rely on, quote, or restate the retracted memo's figures, and
asked that anyone who had passed one outward — to a customer, an insurer, or an auditor — contact
her directly rather than have it corrected by circular.[^s19] She declined to reproduce any figures,
old or corrected, in the retraction itself: "the entire lesson of this episode is that numbers
detach from their context the moment they are convenient."[^s20]

She took her own share of responsibility for how the figures had spread — she had asked at the time
that they be quoted only with their caveat, but some reached slide decks with the caveat trimmed for
space — reasoning that "a number in circulation outlives every caveat attached to it, and the only
reliable caveat is withdrawal," and framing the retraction as keeping a promise she made when she
first issued the estimate in June 2024.[^s21] She confirmed the retraction left KOMET's
audited-and-contractual licence and support figure, and the migration business case built on it,
unaffected, and thanked the audit team "for doing their work thoroughly enough to prove one of my
own memoranda wrong."[^s22][^s23]

## Style profile

- **Puts a precise number on a vague claim, then drives the point home in one line** — "To put a
  number on the standstill: the annual licence and support contracts for KOMET now stand at EUR
  310,000 — for a system whose vendor has not existed for seven years. We pay this every year for
  the privilege of standing still."[^s4]
- **Blunt about limits, with a wry aside** — "If we need more, we go back to the Geschäftsführung —
  and nobody in this room wants that meeting."[^s3]
- **Draws a hard line under a second concession and says so plainly** — "There is no third ask in
  my drawer."[^s14]
- **Turns a personal misstep into a general principle, without flinching from blame** — "I take my
  share of that outcome: a number in circulation outlives every caveat attached to it, and the only
  reliable caveat is withdrawal."[^s21]
- **Closes hard news with dry, self-aware humor** — "That is precisely what audits are for, and it
  is considerably cheaper than learning the same thing from a customer's lawyer."[^s23]
