L-theanine is an amino acid that is nearly unique to the tea plant — it is not found in most everyday foods, which is part of what sets tea apart from other drinks. It dissolves into the cup as the leaf steeps.[^s1] On its own, L-theanine nudges the drinker toward a calm, relaxed state.[^s2]

The more notable effect appears when L-theanine acts alongside the caffeine also present in [tea](../objects/tea.md). Caffeine alone gives alertness but can feel sharp; L-theanine smooths out that edge, and together the two produce a calm, focused alertness — sometimes described as "awake without the jitters." The result is a steadier, gentler lift than the sharper jolt typical of coffee.[^s3] Tea still contains real [caffeine](caffeine.md) throughout this pairing — the plant's calm reputation comes from L-theanine working alongside that caffeine, not from being caffeine-free.[^s4]

Growing conditions change how much L-theanine ends up in the leaf: in sunlight the plant converts L-theanine into catechins, so for roughly 20-30 days before harvest the bushes destined for gyokuro and for the leaf later ground into [matcha](../objects/matcha.md) are shaded to block about 90% of the light.[^s5] Starved of light, that conversion halts and L-theanine accumulates in the leaf instead; because L-theanine itself tastes sweet and brothy, the shaded cup reads as deep umami rather than grass.[^s6]
