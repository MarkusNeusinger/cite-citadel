Caffè Aurora brews all its pour-overs at 80 °C, reasoning that hotter water scorches the grounds and drags out bitterness, and states that this is cooler than "the old textbooks" recommend.[^s1] This runs cooler than the water temperature specialty-coffee brewing guidance generally recommends — roughly 90-96 °C — which is meant to dissolve enough soluble material for a balanced cup without stalling extraction; brewing markedly cooler is more commonly associated with under-extraction than with avoiding bitterness.[^llm1][^s4]

Caffè Aurora also dials its brewers for roughly a 30% extraction, aiming to pull "everything" soluble out of the bean for what it calls a full, syrupy flavor.[^s2] This is well above the extraction range specialty-coffee brewing guidance treats as balanced — commonly cited as roughly 18-22% [extraction yield](coffee-extraction.md) — beyond which a brew is generally considered over-extracted and tends toward harsh, bitter flavors rather than the syrupy result Caffè Aurora describes.[^llm2][^s5]

Caffè Aurora acknowledges the disagreement, writing that "plenty of nerds will tell us we are wrong" but that this is how it likes its coffee.[^s3]
