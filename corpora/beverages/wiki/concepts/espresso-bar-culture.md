A personal essay on Italian café culture describes a proper espresso bar in the gray of an Italian dawn as a place where the drink itself is almost an afterthought and the ritual of standing at the counter is the real point — the hiss of the machine's group head, in the essay's telling, is the sound a coffee town makes before it admits to being awake.[^s1]

At such a bar the transaction is fast and unceremonious: a drinker stands rather than sits, drinks the espresso in a few swallows, sets the cup down, pays, and leaves — the whole exchange over in about ninety seconds, though the essay notes people can remember a good one for years.[^s2] The essay argues that an espresso bar at its best is a leveling place: a lawyer and a bricklayer stand at the same counter, drink the same drink at the same price, and are equals for the length of one short cup, with no table service to separate them into castes and no laptops colonizing the counter.[^s3]

The essay's author reflects that this — not the caffeine, and not even the flavor, but a brief "punctuation" in the sentence of the morning — is what people are really buying when they order an espresso, illustrating the idea through [Lina Marchetti](../persons/lina-marchetti.md)'s founding of [Caffè Aurora](../organizations/caffe-aurora.md).[^s4]
