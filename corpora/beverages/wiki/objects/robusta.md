Robusta, botanically *Coffea canephora*, is one of the two species — alongside [Arabica](arabica.md) — that account for essentially all commercial [coffee](coffee.md) production, and is universally known by its trade name.[^s1] It is a hardier plant that thrives at lower elevations and in warmer, more humid climates than Arabica can tolerate: more resistant to disease and pests, more forgiving of difficult growing conditions, and capable of producing a higher yield per plant, which makes it cheaper to cultivate.[^s2] In the cup, Robusta is generally described as harsher and more bitter, with an earthy or woody character and a heavier body.[^s2] It contains about twice the [caffeine](../concepts/caffeine.md) of Arabica, a difference that contributes both to its more aggressive taste and to its natural resistance to insects, since caffeine acts as a chemical deterrent in the plant.[^s2][^s3] Robusta is widely used in instant coffee and in espresso blends, where it lends body, extra kick, and a thick, stable layer of crema.[^s2][^s3]

See [Coffee](coffee.md) for a side-by-side comparison of Arabica's and Robusta's traits.
