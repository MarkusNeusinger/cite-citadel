*Camellia sinensis* is an evergreen shrub native to the highlands of East and South Asia. It is the single plant species from which every true [tea](tea.md) is made — white, green, oolong, black, and pu-erh alike differ only in how the leaf is processed after picking, not in the plant itself.[^s1][^s4]

## Growing and harvesting

*Camellia sinensis* thrives in warm, humid climates with ample rainfall and good drainage, and many of the finest teas are grown at altitude, where cool nights slow the leaf's growth and concentrate flavour. The plant is kept pruned to a low bush for ease of plucking. The most prized harvest is the "flush" of young shoots — typically the bud and the two leaves immediately below it — which contain the highest proportion of the compounds responsible for aroma and briskness.[^s2]

## Varieties

Two principal varieties dominate cultivation: the China variety is a smaller, hardier plant suited to cooler highland conditions, while the Assam variety, native to South Asia, is larger-leaved and favours hot, lowland tropics. Most commercial teas derive from one of these two or from hybrids between them.[^s3]

Botanically these two are named varieties of the same species: *Camellia sinensis* var. *sinensis* (small-leaf, cold-hardy, native to the China highlands) and var. *assamica* (big-leaf, tropical, malty, the base of Assam tea and of most [pu-erh](pu-erh.md)).[^s5] Beneath these two varieties sit hundreds of named cultivars — specific bred varieties of the plant, the tea equivalent of a grape variety. A few of the best-known are [Yabukita](yabukita.md), the single cultivar behind most Japanese green tea; [Da Hong Pao](da-hong-pao.md), the flagship Wuyi rock oolong; and the bushes behind [Darjeeling](darjeeling.md)'s prized flushes.[^s6]
