In the Himalayan foothills of West Bengal, the same Darjeeling bushes give very different teas by season.[^s1]

## First flush

The first flush (spring, March-April) is light, greenish, floral, and briskly astringent, with no muscatel note.[^s2]

## Second flush

The second flush (early summer, May-June) is the prized harvest: fuller, amber, and carrying the famous muscatel note — a sweet, grape-like, slightly spicy character.[^s3] That muscatel character is partly a gift of the jassid leafhopper, whose nibbling on the warm-season leaf triggers the muscat-grape aromatics; estates now time the second-flush pluck to the insect.[^s4]
