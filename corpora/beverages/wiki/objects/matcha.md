Matcha is green tea that has been dried and milled into a fine powder. Unlike a cup of loose-leaf sencha, it is not steeped and the leaf discarded — the powder is whisked directly into hot water and the whole leaf is drunk, suspended in the cup. Because the whole ground leaf is swallowed rather than just an infusion, a single serving carries more caffeine than the same weight of steeped green tea: roughly 60-70 mg per bowl, about what a modest cup of brewed [coffee](coffee.md) delivers, but with a slower, steadier lift.[^s1]

Matcha comes from the same plant as every other green or black [tea](tea.md), *[Camellia sinensis](camellia-sinensis.md)*. What sets it apart is growing and finishing: the plant is shade-grown for a few weeks before harvest and then stone-ground after drying. The shade pushes the leaf to produce more chlorophyll and more of the amino acids that give matcha its sweet, savory edge, and the stone mill turns the dried leaf into the talc-fine powder that gets whisked.[^s2]

The pre-ground leaf has its own name, tencha: for roughly 20-30 days before harvest the bushes are shaded to block about 90% of the sunlight, the same shading that drives the [L-theanine](../concepts/l-theanine.md), chlorophyll, and caffeine changes behind matcha's umami character.[^s7] Because the whole shaded leaf is whisked and drunk rather than steeped and discarded, all of that concentrated theanine, caffeine, and catechin ends up in the bowl instead of being left behind in a spent leaf.[^s8]

## Grades

Matcha is sold in grades, and the grade decides what the powder is suited for.[^s3]

- **Ceremonial grade** — made from the youngest, tenderest spring leaves, stone-ground slowly so the powder stays cool and fine. It is a bright, almost electric jade green, smooth and sweet with little of the harsh bitterness that scares people off. It is the grade meant to be whisked plain with nothing but water.[^s3]
- **Culinary grade** — made from later harvests and slightly coarser leaf. It is a duller, more olive-toned green that tastes stronger and more astringent. That strength is what lets it carry through milk, sugar, ice, or batter, so it is the grade for lattes, smoothies, ice cream, and baking.[^s4]

A quick quality check at the shop is color: a vivid, saturated green means a higher grade and a fresher milling, while yellowish, brownish, or grey-green powder is old, oxidized, or low quality.[^s5]

## Storage

Matcha is fragile — light, heat, air, and moisture all dull its bright green color and flatten its flavor within weeks. It should be kept sealed, in the dark, somewhere cool, and used within a month or two of opening. Powder that has gone dull and hay-colored has oxidized; it is still safe to drink but tastes flat, and no amount of careful whisking restores it.[^s6]
