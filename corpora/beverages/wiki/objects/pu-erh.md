Pu-erh, from Yunnan, is the [dark tea](../concepts/tea-processing.md) people actually buy — the post-fermented class in which microbes, not the leaf's own enzymes, do the work of transforming it over months to years.[^s1] It splits into two styles: sheng and shou.[^s2]

## Sheng ("raw") pu-erh

Sheng pu-erh is kill-greened, sun-dried into loose "maocha," pressed into cakes, and then left to age slowly — years to decades — through gradual microbial and oxidative maturation. Young sheng is bright, bitter, almost green-tasting, and can be punchy; well-aged sheng turns deep, sweet, and mellow — the style that, like wine, is laid down to improve.[^s3]

## Shou ("ripe") pu-erh

Shou pu-erh is the modern shortcut, developed in 1973 at the Kunming and Menghai tea factories to imitate decades of ageing in a matter of weeks. It uses **wo dui** ("wet piling"): the leaf is heaped damp and warm for roughly 45-60 days so microbes force-ferment it. The result is dark, smooth, earthy, low in bitterness, and drinkable immediately, with no waiting.[^s4]

Same leaf, same province: sheng is aged for years, shou is force-fermented in a pile in weeks — the single most useful distinction to know about pu-erh.[^s5]
