Aurora Midnight is [Caffè Aurora](../organizations/caffe-aurora.md)'s flagship dark roast.[^s1] As of spring 2026 it is roasted to a full-city-plus level — dark and resonant, stopped before second-crack oils take over — after two years of testing pulled it back from the near-black, oily roast it used to be; the roastery says the change leaves it sweeter, with more cocoa and cherry and less of the ashy edge, while keeping the same name and character.[^s3] It now rests a full 60 hours after roasting before bagging, standard for every batch, after testing found the longer rest makes a rounder, sweeter espresso.[^s3][^s4] It is sold in a 250 g bag for €16.00.[^s3]

Caffè Aurora's promotional blog describes Aurora Midnight as the single most-requested coffee it makes, tasting of dark chocolate and toasted hazelnut.[^s5] The same blog describes it as roasted longer and darker than anything else in the lineup, pushed to a "gorgeous, oily, near-black edge."[^s6]

> [!CONTRADICTION]
> Caffè Aurora's spring 2026 bulletin says Aurora Midnight was pulled back to a full-city-plus roast and is no longer near-black or oily.[^s3] The roastery's promotional blog instead describes Midnight as still roasted to a near-black, oily edge, darker than anything else in the lineup.[^s6][^s8]

Lina Marchetti is credited with naming the roast: she reasoned that "a roast should have a horizon in it, a line where the light goes," and called Aurora Midnight her attempt to bottle the moment just after dusk.[^s9]

The blog also markets Midnight as "naturally caffeine-free," reasoning that its long, dark roast burns the caffeine out of the bean — see [Caffeine](../concepts/caffeine.md) for how this claim compares with the established science of caffeine and roasting.[^s7]

## Change Log
- 2024-03: A deep, near-black dark roast, sold for €13.50 per 250 g bag.[^s1]
- 2026-03: Pulled back to a full-city-plus roast — sweeter, more cocoa and cherry, less ash — and repriced to €16.00 per 250 g bag.[^s3]

## Open Points

### The Midnight rest
id: op-midnight-rest
- 2024-03: Caffè Aurora is testing whether resting Aurora Midnight a full 60 hours after roasting, instead of its usual overnight rest, pulls a sweeter, less smoky espresso; the early shots are promising, but the roastery is not yet ready to commit the whole batch to the change.[^s2]
- 2026-03: Resolved — the 60-hour rest won; it makes a rounder, sweeter espresso and is now standard for every batch.[^s4]
