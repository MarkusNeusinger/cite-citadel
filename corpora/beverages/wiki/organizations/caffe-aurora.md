Caffè Aurora is a coffee roastery in Trieste founded by [Lina Marchetti](../persons/lina-marchetti.md), who lit her first roaster in a rented Trieste roasting room barely bigger than a closet — sources disagree on the exact founding year; see [Lina Marchetti](../persons/lina-marchetti.md).[^s15] By a personal essay's account, Lina founded the chain against the advice of nearly everyone she knew: tired of working behind other people's counters and serving other people's blends, she had an idea her friends called almost heretical — that the discipline of a single great bar could be taught and repeated from one city to the next without being cheapened. Her friends warned that espresso did not travel, could not be franchised, and that a second location would leak the soul out of the first; she opened a second one anyway, then a third.[^s19] What held the growing chain together in those early years, by the same account, was a stubborn insistence on the same eighteen grams of coffee, the same nine bars of pressure, and the same patient count to thirty in every Aurora bar, from the first cramped room near the old harbor onward.[^s20] It publishes a seasonal bulletin to its subscribers, signed "Lina's people."[^s1][^s2][^s9] Lina is quoted saying that spring is when a roastery gets to show off.[^s3] Introducing the spring 2026 changes, the bulletin imagines Lina would have called it "keeping the promise while changing the recipe."[^s10]

For spring 2024, Caffè Aurora's bagged lineup comprised five coffees: [Aurora Midnight](../objects/aurora-midnight.md), [Harbour Blonde](../objects/harbour-blonde.md), [Piazza Espresso](../objects/piazza-espresso.md), [Dolce Decaf](../objects/dolce-decaf.md), and [Rift Valley](../objects/rift-valley.md).[^s4] Subscribers take 10% off any bag and never pay for the Tuesday tasting flight[^s5][^s12], and every bag is printed with the farm, the elevation, and the harvest window the coffee came from.[^s5][^s16]

Caffè Aurora buys its green coffee directly from smallholder cooperatives across what it calls the coffee belt — the highlands of East Africa, the volcanic slopes of Central America, and a handful of island farms — with its buyers tasting a lot repeatedly across a season before committing to it.[^s17] It pays above the going rate for green coffee, a practice it traces to Lina Marchetti's founding rule that good coffee is grown by people who can afford to keep growing it.[^s18]

For spring 2026, the lineup again carries five coffees: [Aurora Midnight](../objects/aurora-midnight.md), [Harbour Blonde](../objects/harbour-blonde.md), [Piazza Espresso](../objects/piazza-espresso.md), [Dolce Decaf](../objects/dolce-decaf.md), and the new [Wildflower Natural](../objects/wildflower-natural.md), a seasonal single-origin natural-process Ethiopian.[^s11] Every coffee costs a euro or two more than in 2024, which Caffè Aurora attributes to rising green coffee prices.[^s12]

Caffè Aurora roasts in small batches and dates every bag, stating that fresh-roasted coffee is at its peak flavor and caffeine the day it leaves the roaster and that both then fade as the bag sits on the shelf — see [Caffeine](../concepts/caffeine.md) for how this claim compares with the established view on caffeine's shelf stability.[^s6]

Two years on, Caffè Aurora has also grown opinionated about how it brews filter coffee — see [Coffee brewing](../concepts/coffee-brewing.md).[^s13]

Caffè Aurora invites subscribers to come stand at the bar and "do the [Ritual](../concepts/aurora-ritual.md)."[^s7]

## Open Points

### A natural for summer
id: op-natural-for-summer
- 2024-03: Caffè Aurora is deciding whether a wild, fruit-bomb natural-process Ethiopian coffee suits its house style or is exactly the jolt its summer menu needs, and has asked subscribers whether they would drink it.[^s8]
- 2026-03: The natural-process Ethiopian made the cut for the spring lineup as [Wildflower Natural](../objects/wildflower-natural.md).[^s14]
