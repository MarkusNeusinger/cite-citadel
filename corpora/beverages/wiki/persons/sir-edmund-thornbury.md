Sir Edmund Thornbury was an English trader "with a sharp eye for what the fashionable would pay for," who co-founded the merchant house [Thornbury & Lin](../organizations/thornbury-lin.md) with [Mei Lin](mei-lin.md); the firm first brought tea to England in 1657.[^s1] Within the partnership, Thornbury ran the London end of the business: the warehouses near the river, the buyers among the gentry, and the long negotiations with customs men.[^s1]

Together with Mei Lin, Thornbury took an unusually direct approach to the partnership: rather than dealing at arm's length through middlemen as other traders did, the two shared risk and knowledge directly as equals stretched across half the globe, an arrangement that became the source of the firm's fortune.[^s2]

The firm's own house blog adds that Thornbury was "of restless temperament" with little interest in money for its own sake, drawn instead to the hunt for a thing worth bringing home; it was this instinct that led him to Mei Lin in Canton, who refused to sell him the tea kept for foreigners and sold him good tea instead, at a price that made him laugh and shake her hand.[^s3] The two later married.[^s4]
