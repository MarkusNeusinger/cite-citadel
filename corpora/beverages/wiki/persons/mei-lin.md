Mei Lin was a Canton merchant "of formidable reach who knew the inland tea districts and the men who controlled them," who co-founded the merchant house [Thornbury & Lin](../organizations/thornbury-lin.md) with [Sir Edmund Thornbury](sir-edmund-thornbury.md); the firm first brought tea to England in 1657.[^s1] Within the partnership, Mei Lin worked the Canton waterfront: arranging the chests, judging the grades, and seeing that what was promised in the spring was actually loaded by autumn.[^s1]

Together with Thornbury, Mei Lin took an unusually direct approach to the partnership: rather than dealing at arm's length through middlemen as other traders did, the two shared risk and knowledge directly as equals stretched across half the globe, an arrangement that became the source of the firm's fortune.[^s2]

The firm's own house blog describes Mei Lin as a tea master's daughter from Canton, raised among drying racks and warming woks, whose palate was precise enough that her father trusted her to grade the spring pluck before she could properly read; when Thornbury's ships called at Canton, she refused to sell him the tea reserved for foreigners and sold him good tea instead, at a price that made him laugh and shake her hand.[^s3] The two later married.[^s4] She kept a recipe book, later nicknamed the Green Ledger, in which she recorded not just blends but the moods they were meant for — one for an unshouting morning, one for the afternoon slump, others for grief, celebration, or a quiet winter Sunday; the firm's current blends still trace to that book.[^s5]
