Rosings Park is the Kent residence of [Lady Catherine de Bourgh](../persons/lady-catherine-de-bourgh.md),
separated only by a lane from the garden of Hunsford parsonage, the living held by
[William Collins](../persons/william-collins.md).[^s1] Collins describes its grandeur to Mrs.
Philips at length, including a chimney-piece he says cost eight hundred pounds — an account offered
as his own description, not independently confirmed in this segment.[^s2] Lady Catherine's
daughter, referred to in this segment only as Miss de Bourgh, is its heiress and holds "very
extensive property."[^s3]

That spring the Hunsford party dines at Rosings repeatedly; the dinner is described as "exceedingly
handsome," with a full complement of servants and plate, and the evening's card tables sort the
guests by rank — Lady Catherine, Sir William, and the Collinses at quadrille, while Elizabeth and
Maria Lucas make up a table of cassino with Miss de Bourgh and her companion, Mrs.
Jenkinson.[^s4] Colonel Fitzwilliam and Fitzwilliam
Darcy, Lady Catherine's nephews, arrive to stay at Rosings for Easter.[^s5]
