Netherfield Park is let to [Charles Bingley](../persons/charles-bingley.md) shortly before the
novel opens: a young man of large fortune from the north of England takes it, agreeing with the
owner, Mr. Morris, to take possession before Michaelmas.[^s1] It lies about three miles from
[Longbourn](longbourn.md).[^s2] Bingley had not been of age two years when, on an accidental
recommendation, he looked over the house and grounds for half an hour, was pleased with the
situation and principal rooms, and took it immediately; he holds it only as a tenant, having not
yet purchased an estate of his own.[^s3]

Netherfield is the site of Jane Bennet's illness and convalescence after she is caught in the rain
riding to dine there,[^s4] and of the Netherfield ball.[^s5] By late November the whole Bingley
party — Bingley, his sisters, and Mr. Hurst — leaves for London; per Caroline Bingley's letter to
Jane, none of them intend to return to Hertfordshire that winter.[^s6]

Bingley returns to Netherfield the following autumn to shoot, bringing [Fitzwilliam
Darcy](../persons/fitzwilliam-darcy.md) with him, and resumes his courtship of Jane Bennet
there.[^s7] He and Jane remain at Netherfield only a twelvemonth after their marriage before he buys
an estate in a county neighbouring Derbyshire, judging the proximity to Mrs. Bennet and Meryton
undesirable even to his own easy temper.[^s8]
