_Pride and Prejudice_ is a novel by Jane Austen.[^s1] Its first shape was written around 1796, when
Austen was barely twenty-one; it was revised and finished at Chawton some fifteen years later, and
was not published until 1813, four years before her death, according to the preface to this
edition.[^s2]

This edition is an 1894 printing published by George Allen, of Ruskin House, 156 Charing Cross
Road, London, and printed by the Chiswick Press (Charles Whittingham and Co.), Tooks Court,
Chancery Lane, London.[^s3] It carries a preface by [George Saintsbury](../persons/george-saintsbury.md)
and illustrations by Hugh Thomson, whose illustrations are dedicated "To J. Comyns Carr in
acknowledgment of all I owe to his friendship and advice."[^s4] The edition runs to at least 61
chapters, per its list of illustrations, which indexes chapter headings up to "Chapter LXI" and a
final "The End."[^s5]

## Critical reception (per this edition's preface)

[George Saintsbury](../persons/george-saintsbury.md), writing the preface, states his own critical
judgment that _Pride and Prejudice_ is "the most perfect, the most characteristic, the most
eminently quintessential" of Austen's works, though he judges _Emma_ the more popular of the two
among general readers.[^s6] He argues the book's construction is superior to Austen's other novels,
"almost regular enough for Fielding," with hardly an incident that could be removed without loss to
the story.[^s7] He names Austen's other novels as _Northanger Abbey_, _Persuasion_, _Mansfield
Park_, _Sense and Sensibility_, and _Emma_.[^s8]
