Pemberley is [Fitzwilliam Darcy](../persons/fitzwilliam-darcy.md)'s estate in Derbyshire, worth a
clear ten thousand pounds a year.[^s1] Its library "has been the work of many generations," and
Darcy himself continues to add to it.[^s2]

[George Wickham](../persons/george-wickham.md)'s father managed the Pemberley property as steward
to the late Mr. Darcy, and Wickham was born in the same parish and grew up within the same park as
the present Mr. Darcy, sharing his amusements and upbringing.[^s3] [Mrs. Gardiner](../persons/mrs-gardiner.md),
Elizabeth's aunt, had lived in that part of Derbyshire before her marriage and had seen Pemberley
and known the late Mr. Darcy by reputation.[^s4]

That summer Elizabeth tours the house and grounds with the Gardiners, believing Darcy away; the
housekeeper, [Mrs. Reynolds](../persons/mrs-reynolds.md), shows them the dining parlour, picture
gallery, and a sitting room newly fitted up for Georgiana Darcy's pleasure, and gives an extended,
admiring account of Darcy as landlord, master, and brother.[^s5] Darcy himself arrives a day earlier
than expected and encounters the party in the grounds; he later invites Mr. Gardiner to fish the
stream and invites the whole party, including the newly introduced Georgiana, to dine.[^s6]
Wickham's portrait hangs among the family miniatures in the dining parlour; Mrs. Reynolds identifies
him as the late steward's son who has "turned out very wild."[^s7]

After marrying Darcy, [Elizabeth Bennet](../persons/elizabeth-bennet.md) becomes mistress of
Pemberley, which is now also Georgiana Darcy's home; the sisters grow close there, and
[Mr. Bennet](../persons/mr-bennet.md) becomes a frequent visitor, especially when least
expected.[^s8] Though Darcy can never receive [George Wickham](../persons/george-wickham.md) at
Pemberley, [Lydia Bennet](../persons/lydia-bennet.md) is occasionally a visitor there when her
husband is elsewhere, and even [Lady Catherine de Bourgh](../persons/lady-catherine-de-bourgh.md),
after their estrangement over the marriage, eventually condescends to visit.[^s9]
