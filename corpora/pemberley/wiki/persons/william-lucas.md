Sir William Lucas had formerly been in trade in Meryton, where he made "a tolerable fortune" and
was knighted after addressing the king during his mayoralty; the honour led him to give up his
business and remove with his family to Lucas Lodge, about a mile from Meryton.[^s1] He is courteous
and sociable, "all attention to everybody," and proud of his rank.[^s2] His wife, Lady Lucas, is
described as "a very good kind of woman, not too clever," a valuable neighbour to
[Mrs. Bennet](mrs-bennet.md); the couple have several children, including
[Charlotte Lucas](charlotte-lucas.md).[^s3]

At the Netherfield ball he compliments [Fitzwilliam Darcy](fitzwilliam-darcy.md) on his dancing and
alludes to an expected match between [Jane Bennet](jane-bennet.md) and
[Charles Bingley](charles-bingley.md).[^s4] He personally calls at Longbourn to announce Charlotte's
engagement to [William Collins](william-collins.md).[^s5]

Sir William stays a week at Hunsford, during which Collins drives him about the country each morning
in his gig; his stay is enough to convince him of Charlotte's comfortable establishment, though the
grandeur of Rosings leaves even him awed into near-silence.[^s6]

On the Collinses' visit to Lucas Lodge after Elizabeth's engagement, Sir William compliments Darcy
on "carrying away the brightest jewel of the country" and voices hopes of meeting the family
frequently at St. James's; Darcy bears the obsequious civility with outward calm, though he shrugs
once Sir William is out of sight.[^s7]
