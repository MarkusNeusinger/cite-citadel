Jane Austen is the author of [Pride and Prejudice](../objects/pride-and-prejudice.md).[^s1] Per the
preface to this edition, she wrote the novel's first draft around 1796, when she was "barely
twenty-one," then revised and finished it at Chawton about fifteen years later; it was published in
1813, four years before her death.[^s2] The preface's author, [George Saintsbury](george-saintsbury.md),
states that in two short periods of writing — one of about three years, another of not much more
than five — Austen "executed six capital works, and has not left a single failure," an assessment
he offers as his own opinion.[^s3]

Saintsbury names her other novels as _Northanger Abbey_, _Persuasion_, _Mansfield Park_, _Sense and
Sensibility_, and _Emma_.[^s4] He credits her, as a stylistic hallmark, with building up a picture
through "myriad, trivial, unforced strokes," illustrating the point with two touches from _Pride
and Prejudice_: that Mr. Collins changed his mind from Jane to Elizabeth Bennet "while Mrs. Bennet
was stirring the fire," and that Mr. Darcy "brought his coffee-cup back himself."[^s5]
