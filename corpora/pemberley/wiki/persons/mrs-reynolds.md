Mrs. Reynolds is the housekeeper at [Pemberley](../objects/pemberley.md), described as "a
respectable looking elderly woman, much less fine, and more civil" than Elizabeth Bennet
expects.[^s1] She shows the Gardiners and Elizabeth through the house, pointing out the dining
parlour, the picture gallery, and a sitting room recently refitted for Georgiana Darcy's
pleasure.[^s2]

She identifies a miniature of [George Wickham](george-wickham.md) as the son of her late master's
steward, brought up at his expense, now "gone into the army" but "turned out very wild."[^s3] Of her
master, [Fitzwilliam Darcy](fitzwilliam-darcy.md), she gives extensive, unprompted praise: she has
"never had a cross word from him" in her life and has known him since he was four years old, calls
him "the best landlord, and the best master, that ever lived," and says some call him proud but she
has "never seen anything of it."[^s4] She reports that Darcy is not at Pemberley as much as she could
wish but spends about half his time there, and that [Georgiana Darcy](georgiana-darcy.md) is always
down for the summer months, plays and sings all day, and has just received a new instrument as a
gift from her brother.[^s5]
