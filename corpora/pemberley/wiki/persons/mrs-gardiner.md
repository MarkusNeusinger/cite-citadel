Mrs. Gardiner is married to [Mrs. Bennet](mrs-bennet.md)'s brother, Mr. Gardiner, "a sensible,
gentlemanlike man" who lives by trade in London, near his own warehouses.[^s1] Several years
younger than Mrs. Bennet, she is "an amiable, intelligent, elegant woman," and a great favourite of
[Jane](jane-bennet.md) and [Elizabeth Bennet](elizabeth-bennet.md), with whom she corresponds and
who have often stayed with her in town.[^s2]

Before her marriage she lived for a considerable time in the part of Derbyshire near
[Pemberley](../objects/pemberley.md), and had seen the house and known the late Mr. Darcy by
reputation; she recalls having heard the young [Fitzwilliam Darcy](fitzwilliam-darcy.md) spoken of
as "a very proud, ill-natured boy."[^s3] The Gardiners spend Christmas at Longbourn and invite Jane
to stay with them afterward in London.[^s4] Mrs. Gardiner cautions Elizabeth against encouraging an
attachment to [George Wickham](george-wickham.md) given his want of fortune; Elizabeth promises to
be prudent.[^s5]

That summer Mrs. Gardiner and her husband take Elizabeth on a tour that, curtailed by Mr. Gardiner's
business, is redirected from the Lakes to Derbyshire, with a stay at Lambton, near her former
home.[^s6] She presses to revisit Pemberley for the beauty of its grounds, and afterward, struck by
the housekeeper's praise of Darcy, wonders aloud to Elizabeth whether they have been "deceived" in
their earlier opinion of him.[^s7] She remains at Longbourn with her children through the worst of
the Lydia crisis, before returning to London as Mr. Bennet comes home.[^s8]

In response to Elizabeth's request for an explanation, Mrs. Gardiner writes a long letter setting
out, in full, Darcy's role in finding Wickham and Lydia and arranging their marriage, sworn to
secrecy by Darcy himself as to the money he spent.[^s9] In the letter she confesses she has come to
like Darcy very much, praising his understanding and manners as fully as pleasing as she found them
in Derbyshire, and gently teases Elizabeth for never once mentioning his name in her own
letters.[^s10]
