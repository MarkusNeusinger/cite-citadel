Mrs. Younge was engaged as companion to [Georgiana Darcy](georgiana-darcy.md) at Ramsgate, and was
dismissed from that charge, per [Fitzwilliam Darcy](fitzwilliam-darcy.md)'s letter to Elizabeth,
after being found complicit in [George Wickham](george-wickham.md)'s scheme to elope with his then
fifteen-year-old sister.[^s1]

Per Mrs. Gardiner's letter to Elizabeth, Mrs. Younge afterward took a large house in Edward Street,
London, and maintained herself by letting lodgings; she remained intimately acquainted with Wickham,
who lodged with her on his first arrival in London with Lydia Bennet, and would have taken the
couple into her own house had she been able.[^s2] When Darcy went to her for information about
Wickham's whereabouts, she would not betray her friend's trust "without bribery and corruption,"
and it was two or three days before Darcy could get from her the direction he wanted.[^s2]
