Georgiana Darcy is [Fitzwilliam Darcy](fitzwilliam-darcy.md)'s sister, about fifteen or sixteen
years old.[^s1] Since their father's death her home has been London, where a lady lives with her
and superintends her education.[^s2] [Caroline Bingley](caroline-bingley.md) and
[Louisa Hurst](louisa-hurst.md) praise her accomplishments effusively, including her progress on
the harp, and Caroline writes to [Jane Bennet](jane-bennet.md) hoping Georgiana will become her
sister by marrying [Charles Bingley](charles-bingley.md).[^s3]

Per [George Wickham](george-wickham.md)'s account, Georgiana was affectionate and pleasing as a
child, and fond of him, but is "too much like her brother, — very, very proud" as she has grown
up — an assessment offered as Wickham's own opinion, given as part of an account this segment does
not independently confirm.[^s4]

Darcy's letter to Elizabeth reveals that last summer, while lodged at Ramsgate under the care of her
companion Mrs. Younge, Georgiana — then fifteen — was persuaded by Wickham, in league with Mrs.
Younge, to believe herself in love with him and to agree to elope, chiefly for the sake of her
thirty-thousand-pound fortune. Darcy joined them unexpectedly a day or two before the planned
elopement, and Georgiana, unable to bear the thought of grieving him, confessed the whole scheme
herself; Wickham left immediately and Mrs. Younge was dismissed.[^s5] [Colonel Fitzwilliam](colonel-fitzwilliam.md)
shares her guardianship with her brother.[^s6]

That summer, having reached [Pemberley](../objects/pemberley.md) only that morning, Georgiana calls
on Elizabeth at Lambton the very day of her arrival; Elizabeth finds her not proud, as rumoured, but
exceedingly shy, scarcely able to say more than a monosyllable.[^s7] Her brother visibly wishes the
two to become friends and is anxious for his sister to be liked.[^s8]

On hearing of her brother's engagement to Elizabeth, Georgiana's joy is as sincere as his own; four
sides of paper are insufficient to contain her delight and her eagerness to be loved by her new
sister.[^s9] Once Elizabeth becomes mistress of Pemberley, the two grow close: Georgiana, who had
always regarded her brother with something bordering on awe, learns from Elizabeth's lively,
teasing manner toward him "that a woman may take liberties with her husband" a brother would not
allow a younger sister.[^s10]
