Anne de Bourgh, referred to throughout as "Miss de Bourgh," is the only daughter of
[Lady Catherine de Bourgh](lady-catherine-de-bourgh.md) and the late Sir Lewis de Bourgh, and
heiress of [Rosings Park](../objects/rosings-park.md).[^s1] William Collins calls her sickly and of
"very extensive property"; when Elizabeth Bennet first sees her arrive at the parsonage gate, she is
startled by how "thin and small" she is, with pale, sickly features that are "not plain" but
"insignificant," and she speaks little beyond a low word to her companion, Mrs. Jenkinson.[^s2]

Lady Catherine intends her for [Fitzwilliam Darcy](fitzwilliam-darcy.md), her nephew, speaking of
the match as though it would "unite the two estates," and repeatedly claims Anne would have been "a
delightful performer" at the piano had her health allowed her to learn.[^s3] Anne herself gives no
sign of particular feeling toward Darcy in his presence.[^s4] At the party's departure from Kent, she
"exerts herself" to courtesy and shake hands with both Elizabeth and Maria Lucas, a notable warmth
from someone otherwise reserved.[^s5]

Confronting Elizabeth Bennet over a rumoured Darcy engagement, Lady Catherine describes Anne's match
with Darcy as settled "from their infancy," planned "while in their cradles" by both mothers — a
claim Elizabeth challenges as never binding on Darcy by either honour or inclination.[^s6] Darcy goes
on to marry Elizabeth instead.[^s7]
