Louisa Hurst is [Charles Bingley](charles-bingley.md)'s sister, "who had married a man of more
fashion than fortune."[^s1] Alongside [Caroline Bingley](caroline-bingley.md), the narrator
describes her as proud and conceited, sharing Caroline's disdain for the Bennets' connections and
her jealousy of [Elizabeth Bennet](elizabeth-bennet.md) with Darcy.[^s2] She sings duets with
Caroline at Netherfield in the evenings.[^s3]

Her husband, Mr. Hurst, is "an indolent man, who lived only to eat, drink, and play at cards"; when
cards are not offered one evening he stretches out on a sofa and falls asleep.[^s4] He offers only a
"slight bow" and a "very glad" upon Jane Bennet's convalescence, in contrast to his brother-in-law
Bingley's evident concern.[^s5]

That summer she is present, with Caroline Bingley, when Elizabeth and the Gardiners call on
Georgiana Darcy at Pemberley.[^s6]
