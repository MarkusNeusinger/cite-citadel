Catherine Bennet, called Kitty, is scolded by her father for having "no discretion in her coughs"
after she coughs repeatedly at breakfast.[^s1] She and [Lydia Bennet](lydia-bennet.md) are the two
sisters most often walking to [Meryton](../misc/meryton.md) to see the militia officers and their
aunt, Mrs. Philips.[^s2] She is present when [George Wickham](george-wickham.md) is first
introduced to the family in the street at Meryton, and later follows Elizabeth and Jane out of the
room during the household's uproar over Elizabeth's refusal of [William Collins](william-collins.md).[^s3]

Kitty is left behind when Lydia is invited to Brighton, and resents it bitterly, protesting she has
as much right to go as her younger sister.[^s4] It later emerges that she had known of Lydia's
growing attachment to Wickham for weeks from her letters, but kept the confidence.[^s5]

When Bingley returns to Netherfield with Darcy, Kitty is the first to recognize Darcy from the
window, "that tall, proud man" she remembers seeing before.[^s6] On the walk where Elizabeth and
Darcy become engaged, Kitty accompanies them only as far as Lucas Lodge, too much in awe of Darcy to
talk.[^s7]

By the novel's close, Kitty spends most of her time with her two elder sisters and, removed from
Lydia's influence, becomes "less irritable, less ignorant, and less insipid"; her father never
permits her to visit the Wickhams despite Lydia's invitations.[^s8]
