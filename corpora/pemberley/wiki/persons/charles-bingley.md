Charles Bingley is a young man of large fortune from the north of England who leases
[Netherfield Park](../objects/netherfield-park.md) at the novel's opening.[^s1] He inherited nearly
a hundred thousand pounds from his father, who had intended but not lived to purchase an estate;
Bingley himself has not yet purchased one, and holds Netherfield only as a tenant.[^s2] He is
close friends with [Fitzwilliam Darcy](fitzwilliam-darcy.md) despite an "opposition of character":
the narrator describes Bingley as easy, open, and ductile in temper, sure of being liked wherever he
goes, in contrast to Darcy's haughtiness.[^s3]

At the Meryton assembly he dances with [Jane Bennet](jane-bennet.md) twice and calls her the most
beautiful creature he ever beheld.[^s4] When Jane falls ill at Netherfield, his attentions to her
are marked; he is the only member of the Netherfield party [Elizabeth Bennet](elizabeth-bennet.md)
regards with any complacency during her stay.[^s5] He hosts the Netherfield ball at
[Lydia Bennet](lydia-bennet.md)'s prompting.[^s6] By late November he leaves for London with his
sisters and Darcy; per [Caroline Bingley](caroline-bingley.md)'s letters, he shows no sign of
returning to Hertfordshire that winter.[^s7]

That summer Bingley accompanies Darcy and Georgiana Darcy to Lambton to call on Elizabeth, greeting
her with unaffected warmth; he remarks that it has been eight months, precisely since the 26th of
November, since he last saw Jane at the Netherfield ball, and asks whether all her sisters are still
at Longbourn.[^s8] He and Georgiana join Darcy's invitation for the Gardiners and Elizabeth to dine
at Pemberley.[^s9]

Bingley returns to Netherfield to shoot, bringing Darcy with him; he calls at Longbourn and is
invited, with Darcy, to dine there some days later.[^s10] He calls again a few days after, alone,
and is engineered by Mrs. Bennet into a private morning with Jane, during which he proposes and is
accepted.[^s11] Darcy later tells Elizabeth that Bingley had never suspected his own friend's earlier
interference until Darcy confessed it the evening before leaving for London — a confession that
angered Bingley only until he was certain of Jane's continuing regard.[^s12] From this time Bingley
becomes a daily visitor at Longbourn.[^s13]

By the novel's close, he and Jane remain at Netherfield only a twelvemonth before he buys an estate
in a county neighbouring Derbyshire, gratifying his sisters' wish to be near Pemberley.[^s14]
