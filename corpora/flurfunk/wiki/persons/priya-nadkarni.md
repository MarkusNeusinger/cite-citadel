Priya Nadkarni is the founder and CEO of [Larkspur](../organizations/larkspur.md).[^s1] Before
founding Larkspur, she worked on analytics infrastructure at larger companies; she has said she
kept hitting the same wall — everything called itself "real-time," but nothing actually felt
real-time. She and a small founding group set out to build the product they wished existed,
founding Larkspur in 2021 in Lisbon; that became [Skylight](../systems/skylight.md).[^s2]

On The Build Loop podcast (2026-08-28), Nadkarni said Larkspur has 28 people, calling the team
deliberately small; she said the company has stayed lean on purpose, and that she thinks the
pressure of staying lean is part of why the product is good.[^s3]

On the same podcast, Nadkarni said "we're the only ones who do it right" and that Larkspur "is the
only real-time analytics platform with true sub-second refresh," arguing rival products are "doing
polling and calling it streaming."[^s4][^s5] This is the founder's own competitive claim about her
company's product; it is not independently verified against other analytics platforms in this
wiki.[^llm1]

She also said Larkspur has been profitable since its first month and has never taken a "lose money
to grow" approach, adding that the company has never had a month in the red.[^s6][^s7] This is a
financial claim from the company's own founder, not independently verified in this wiki.[^llm2]

On the general availability of [Skylight](../systems/skylight.md) 2.0 on 1 April 2026, Nadkarni
said: "Skylight 2.0 is the version we always wanted to ship. Bringing it to three regions means
teams around the world get the same real-time experience close to home."[^s1]

## Style profile

- Warm, informal opener and closer with an interviewer: "Thanks Alex, big fan of the show. Happy to
  be here."[^s8] ... "Thank you, Alex. This was fun."[^s9]
- Confident, and doubles down rather than hedges when a claim is challenged: "It is, and I'll stand
  behind it."[^s5]
- Uses short, fragmentary emphasis instead of a full sentence to underline a point: "First
  month."[^s6]
- Returns to one core phrase as a refrain when describing the company's mission: "the north star
  doesn't change: real-time that's actually real-time."[^s10]
