The Ferrick Tide Clock is the [Instrument Works](../organizations/instrument-works.md)' name for
the tide-clock pattern that has served the harbour tower at
[Port Ilvess](../misc/port-ilvess.md) since 1963 — the
[Port Ilvess Harbour Tower Clock](port-ilvess-harbour-tower-clock.md), built by
[Tomas Ferrick](../persons/tomas-ferrick.md).[^s1] By the Works' 1992 catalogue it is in its
fourth series, taken up two years after the Institute's 1988 remove to Corran Hill, and is
offered, as ever, in the harbour size and the house size.[^s1]

## Design and delivery

The movement is Ferrick's own double-detent escapement, with the tidal train geared for the
purchaser's home port; ports of "unusual temper" — the Fell Rocks among them — are respectfully
declined.[^s2] Each clock is run at the Works against the transit series for one full lunation
before delivery, and its rate card is packed with it.[^s3]

## Pricing and waiting list

The harbour size is priced at two hundred and forty skells, the house size at ninety skells, and
the waiting list for either stands at just over a year; the catalogue notes the Works has "buried
harbour boards and outlived their successors" and recommends ordering early.[^s4]
