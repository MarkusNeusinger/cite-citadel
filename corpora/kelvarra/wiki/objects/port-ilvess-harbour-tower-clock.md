The Port Ilvess Harbour Tower Clock is the tide clock [Tomas Ferrick](../persons/tomas-ferrick.md)
built for the harbour tower at [Port Ilvess](../misc/port-ilvess.md), begun on 14 March 1961 and
set going on 4 March 1963.[^s1][^s7] Ferrick's own workshop day-book, kept at his bench through the
build, calls the finished instrument the "harbour size."[^s6]

## Design and construction

Ferrick cleared his bench and began the clock in earnest on 14 March 1961, after the harbour board
had put the job off for two years. [Maren Aldervane](../persons/maren-aldervane.md) came down at
noon to see the empty bench and said only "good" — "as much ceremony as this house runs to," by
Ferrick's account, "and more than I wanted."[^s1]

Brass for the clock came from mainland founders at forty ferlings the hundredweight, plus six
ferlings carriage — Ferrick judged it "worth it," markedly better than an earlier lot from the
harbour chandler that had "machined like cheese."[^s2]

By May 1961 Ferrick had identified the core design problem: the escapement carrying the tidal train
with no way for "the tidal train telling the escapement about the weather" — a problem his third
sketch resolved with a double-detent escapement.[^s3] He ran that escapement dry on the bench
through a full lunation of a model tidal train in February 1962 and found no dropped beat. Aldervane
watched the trial for the better part of an hour without a word before saying it would do — a
verdict Ferrick called "a citation from the Republic" from so sparing a source of praise. The
tower's wheel castings were then ordered for sixty-two ferlings, the founder demanding half in
advance.[^s4]

The tidal gearing itself was cut in June 1962, to arithmetic supplied by the
[Brann Tidal Survey](../projects/brann-tidal-survey.md) — arithmetic Ferrick trusted "as I trust
the tide itself" even as cutting a wheel to it proved "another church": fourteen leaves were recut
before the tooth count and his temper agreed, the count itself having been right both times.[^s5]

## Proving and installation

By 15 January 1963 the harbour size stood complete on the bench. Its own proving began the
following Monday against the transit series, run for one full lunation; Ferrick refused to shorten
it despite weekly pressure from the harbour board, noting that "the board would ask the tide to
hurry if it thought the tide had a council it answered to."[^s6]

The clock was set going in the harbour tower on 4 March 1963, with no speeches — the board was kept
off by weather Ferrick thanked for it. Aldervane climbed the tower with him to watch the tidal
train's first change and said that "the tables promise and the harbour performs," and that a clock
siding with the harbour was worth the wait; the two then had tea at the counter "like any two
islanders with a watch between them." The rate card went up on the tower door the following
Monday.[^s7]

Over its first six months, proved against the transit series through the season's storms, the
clock's rate held closely enough that the
[Brann Tidal Survey](../projects/brann-tidal-survey.md) stopped writing Ferrick about it — "the
highest honour the Republic has yet paid me," in his own estimate.[^s8]

## Later production

The pattern went on to become the [Instrument Works](../organizations/instrument-works.md)'
standard tide-clock product — [The Ferrick Tide Clock](the-ferrick-tide-clock.md) — offered in the
harbour and house sizes; by the Works' 1992 catalogue it was in its fourth series, taken up two
years after the Institute's 1988 remove to Corran Hill.[^s9]
