Saltglass is the islanders' name for the dark natural glass of the
[Republic of Kelvarra](../misc/republic-of-kelvarra.md): a volcanic glass, black as a wet slate
and glossier, that the old eruptions of [Mount Sarn](../misc/mount-sarn.md) poured out in sheets
and seams long before anyone lived in the islands to name it.[^s1] Sarn has been quiet through the
whole of recorded history, but its old flows ring the island underwater and above it, and the sea
has been quarrying them ever since.[^s2] The sea does the work no lapidary would take on: it breaks
the seams, rolls the shards for centuries in the boulder strands, and delivers them up the beach as
pebbles — smooth, black, cold to the hand, and holding a green-edged gleam in the light that no
other stone of the islands offers.[^s3]

## Where it is found

The best saltglass comes ashore on Sarn itself, on the storm beaches below the old flows, where the
wrack line can glitter with it after an equinoctial gale.[^s4] [Ilvess](../misc/port-ilvess.md)
gets its share on the western strands at Corrie Ness, and Little Moriny a little; the outer
skerries get the most of all, though the boatmen say they are the least worth the sail to
reach.[^s5] A good piece is judged the way pearls are judged, by depth and evenness: a pebble of
true black with the green edge and no grey bloom will fetch more in the
[Port Ilvess](../misc/port-ilvess.md) fish market than the fish do.[^s6]

## Uses and custom

Saltglass is reckoned Kelvarra's jewel. It is cut and polished for ring stones and brooches, strung
rough for luck, and set by long custom into the lintel of a new house — one pebble for each soul
under the roof, so that an islander reading a doorway is, in effect, taking a census.[^s7] A bride
wears a piece of it, a boat carries a piece in the keel, and the lamp room of every light in the
archipelago holds a pebble of saltglass on the sill, put there by generations of keepers "for
reasons no two of them give alike."[^s8]

## Properties

Fresh-broken saltglass is sharp beyond any island metal; the middens on Sarn show blades and
scrapers of it going back to the first settlement, and the shard beds below the flows are, in
effect, the Republic's oldest tool works.[^s9] The prized green edge is a thin-section effect: the
glass is not truly black but a deep bottle-dark, as a thin sliver held to the sun will show.[^s10]
The grey bloom that ruins a pebble for the jeweller is the slow work of the salt itself, the sea
taking back — molecule by molecule — what it spent centuries polishing.[^s11]

## Study and supply

Geologists at the
[Aldervane Institute for Natural Measurement](../organizations/aldervane-institute-for-natural-measurement.md)
map Sarn's old flows and keep an eye on the shard beds, and are said to grade their own fieldwork
by the pocketfuls of saltglass they bring home; the essay's author reports having seen the
Institute's specimen drawers on Corran Hill, and remarks that in Kelvarra, science and beachcombing
are not seriously distinguished.[^s12]

Whether the supply is endless is a question the islanders decline to ask aloud. The sea has been
generous for as long as the wrack line has been walked and the old flows are large, though Sarn's
hot springs are read by some as a hint the mountain is not entirely done making more.[^s13] The
essay concludes that a visitor who buys a pebble in the fish market and hears the price will
correctly conclude that the sea is not so generous as all that.[^s14]
