The Brann Tidal Survey (BTS) is the
[Aldervane Institute for Natural Measurement](../organizations/aldervane-institute-for-natural-measurement.md)'s
tide-gauge survey of the [Republic of Kelvarra](../misc/republic-of-kelvarra.md), maintained from
nine stations around the archipelago.[^s1] Its 1987 season report records the Survey's
thirty-fifth consecutive season, with all nine stations reporting and no loss of record at any of
them — a result the Survey says it does not take for granted.[^s2] The season opens with the
proving of the gauges in April and closes with the equinoctial series in late September.[^s2]

## Stations

As of 1987, the nine stations stood: three about the harbour and roads of
[Port Ilvess](../misc/port-ilvess.md); two on the west coast of Ilvess, at Corrie Ness and the
Long Skerry; two on Sarn, at Sarnholt and the Hot Spring landing; one on Little Moriny, at the
light; and the outer station on the Fell Rocks, described that year as the most expensive item in
the Survey's budget per foot of paper it produces.[^s3]

## Method

Each April the Survey's gauges are proved against a travelling standard, which is itself proved
against the [KSB](../objects/ksb.md) before it leaves the building and again on its return; the
Survey's pressures are reduced against the KSB series, and its heights are given on the Survey
datum of 1953.[^s4]

The Survey's tidal arithmetic also went into the harbour clock itself: Tomas Ferrick's own workshop
day-book records cutting the
[Port Ilvess Harbour Tower Clock](../objects/port-ilvess-harbour-tower-clock.md)'s tidal gearing to
the Survey's figures in June 1962, trusting them completely even as the cutting cost him fourteen
recut leaves.[^s13]

## The 1987 season

The tidal year was unremarkable in range and remarkable in temper: the spring series ran close to
prediction all season, seldom off by more than a hand's breadth at any harbour station, while the
equinoctial gales drove two storm surges through the roads in September, the greater of which put
the fish market arcades under water for four hours.[^s5] The Survey copied its surge sheets to the
harbour board along with its renewed recommendation on the height of the north quay — a
recommendation the board had, by 1987, declined for eleven consecutive years, "with unfailing
courtesy."[^s6]

The harbour tide clock — the
[Port Ilvess Harbour Tower Clock](../objects/port-ilvess-harbour-tower-clock.md), built by
[Tomas Ferrick](../persons/tomas-ferrick.md) — was proved daily
against the transit series through the whole season and kept its rate within four seconds in the
month — a performance the Survey says no instrument of its kind on the mainland approaches. Over
the season the tower housing the clock was struck twice by gulls and once, during the September
surge, by a fish.[^s7]

The Survey's levelling party, extending the datum inland for the harbour board's drainage scheme,
carried the levels up the Sarnholt track and closed its triangle on the summit of
[Mount Sarn](../misc/mount-sarn.md), whose height it computed at 2,315 metres above the Survey
datum. The computation was checked twice and the field books were clean; the Survey notes only
that the theodolite was proved on the transit pillar before and after the season, and that
"mountains do not read almanacs."[^s8]

> [!CONTRADICTION]
> The Survey's 1987 levelling places Mount Sarn's summit at 2,315 metres above the Survey
> datum, a result it reports checking twice with clean field books.[^s8] The *Almanac of
> Kelvarra* instead gives 2,290 metres.[^s12]

[Mrs H. Corliss](../persons/mrs-h-corliss.md) retired in 1987 after twenty-two seasons keeping the
Little Moriny station; her granddaughter took up the station book with the new season.[^s9]

## Plans for the coming year

For the following season the Survey proposed no change of station and one change of method —
converting the Fell Rocks gauge to the sealed recording pattern, if the
[Institute](../organizations/aldervane-institute-for-natural-measurement.md)'s
[Instrument Works](../organizations/instrument-works.md) could spare one before the autumn — alongside its customary request to the Council for a second
computer: by which, as every year since the request was first entered, the Survey means a person
who computes.[^s10]

The 1987 report was prepared at the Old Customs House, Port Ilvess, for the Council of the
Institute, and submitted on 30 October 1987.[^s1][^s11]
