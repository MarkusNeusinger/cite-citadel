The Aldervane Institute for Natural Measurement (AINM) is the
[Republic of Kelvarra](../misc/republic-of-kelvarra.md)'s scientific
establishment.[^s1] By its founder's account, it was, in its first year, the whole of natural
measurement in the Republic.[^s2]

## Founding

The harbour board of [Port Ilvess](../misc/port-ilvess.md) owned the Old Customs House, could not
afford to heat it, and let [Maren Aldervane](../persons/maren-aldervane.md) use its two driest
rooms on a handshake. In the spring of 1949 she moved her trunk of instruments in and screwed a
brass plate to the door, and, by her account, the Institute existed from that point.[^s2]

> [!CONTRADICTION]
> Aldervane's own memoir dates the Institute's founding to spring 1949, when she moved into the
> Old Customs House and fixed a brass plate to its door.[^s2] The *Almanac of Kelvarra* instead
> states she founded the Institute in 1952.[^s1]

Aldervane wrote the Institute's charter that same summer, at her kitchen table. Alongside the
ordinary provisions for a council, an audit, and a winding-up clause, she added three of her own:
that the Institute answer for its numbers to nobody but the numbers; that any islander could have
a weight or watch proved for the price of the tea drunk while waiting;[^s13] and a permanent ban on the
Institute patenting any instrument it makes, on the grounds that "a standard you may not freely
copy is not a standard, it is a toll gate." The Institute's lawyers warned the anti-patent clause
would ruin it; the firm no longer exists, and the Institute does.[^s3]

## Growth

In 1958 Aldervane hired [Tomas Ferrick](../persons/tomas-ferrick.md), a harbour clockmaker's son,
who built the clock in the Port Ilvess harbour tower — the
[Port Ilvess Harbour Tower Clock](../objects/port-ilvess-harbour-tower-clock.md) — and stayed at
the Institute for the rest of his working life. Aldervane credits Ferrick and the other staff — working "at benches, slowly" —
with having built the Institute, rather than herself.[^s4]

She directed the Institute until her retirement and, by her own account, watched it grow past what
she had imagined at its founding, "with a founder's usual mixture of pride and indignation."[^s5][^s12]
She set her stamp on the Institute so thoroughly that islanders still call it simply "Maren's
house."[^s12]

## Standards and reach

The Institute kept the Old Customs House at Port Ilvess for its first decades before removing, in
1988, to a purpose-built campus on Corran Hill above the town, where it remains.[^s7][^s14] It keeps the
national standards of weight, length, and pressure;[^s8] the Republic's weights and measures
generally have been metric, kept, proved, and certified since mid-century by the Institute, whose
standards are the law of the Republic.[^s9] It also maintains gauges for the
[Brann Tidal Survey](../projects/brann-tidal-survey.md) (BTS) at nine stations
around the archipelago,[^s10] and its [Instrument Works](instrument-works.md) supplies barometers
and chronometers of a quality the mainland pays dearly for.[^s11] Its geologists map
[Mount Sarn](../misc/mount-sarn.md)'s old flows and keep an eye on the
[saltglass](../objects/saltglass.md) shard beds, and keep specimen drawers of it on Corran
Hill.[^s15]

## The Primer

The Institute's *Aldervane Primer of Natural Measurement* is the standard first text of the
Institute and of every school in the Republic; its sixth edition (1994) reprints Aldervane's
original foreword to the first edition in full.[^s6]
