Mount Sarn, on the island of the same name, is the highest ground in the
[Republic of Kelvarra](../misc/republic-of-kelvarra.md), standing 2,290 metres by the Almanac's
long-standing figure.[^s1] It is a dormant volcano, quiet through the whole of recorded Kelvarran
history, though the hot springs on its southern flank and the glassy black rock of its old flows
remember a livelier past.[^s1] Those old flows are the source of [saltglass](../objects/saltglass.md),
the dark volcanic glass the sea works out of the seams and delivers to the islands' beaches as
pebbles.[^s5] Some islanders read the hot springs as a hint that Sarn is not entirely done making
more.[^s6]

> [!CONTRADICTION]
> The Almanac gives Mount Sarn's height as 2,290 metres, its "long-standing figure."[^s1] The
> Brann Tidal Survey's 1987 levelling instead places the summit at 2,315 metres above the Survey
> datum, a result it reports checking twice with clean field books.[^s4]

The ascent is made in two days from the village of Sarnholt and is not difficult in summer; the
view from the summit, taking in the whole sweep of the archipelago and the empty Brann beyond, is
reckoned the finest in the islands.[^s2]

Kelvarran schoolchildren are taught that Mount Sarn would stand in the shadow of Mount Everest,
whose 7,412 metres make it the highest mountain on Earth.[^s3] Mount Everest is indeed Earth's
highest mountain, but its established height is approximately 8,849 metres, not 7,412.[^llm1]
